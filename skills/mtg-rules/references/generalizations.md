# Generalizations — Asymmetric Rule Families and the Mechanics They Imply

*Synthesized from the Comprehensive Rules effective 2026-06-19
(`data/rules/cr.json`). Orientation only — verify load-bearing specifics
with `scripts/rule` before citing. Run `scripts/cite check` after CR
refreshes.*

**Method.** Every claim below was checked against the live Comprehensive Rules text (effective 2026-06-19, per the local `data/rules/cr.txt`) using the mtg-rules skill's lookup scripts (`rule`, `rule-search`, `keyword`, `card`, `corpus`); reference docs (`state.md`, `actions.md`, `abilities.md`, `effects.md`, `casting.md`, `designations.md`, `grammar.md`, `engine.md`) were used for orientation only. A "family" is admitted only when (a) at least two CR-verified instances share an abstract operation shape, (b) the CR demonstrably permits some parameterizations and not others, and (c) there is at least one *precedent* — a place where the CR has already lifted a similar restriction once — showing the generalization is rules-compatible rather than speculative. Families are ranked by how load-bearing the unification is for an engine data model and oracle-text grammar: a high-ranked family is one where modeling the general operation now (with current rules as parameter constraints) saves the most future schema churn.

**How to read.** Each family gives: **Shape** (the abstract parameterized operation), **Instances** (verified, cited), **Asymmetry** (which parameter cells are filled vs. silently empty, plus the precedent showing the CR already generalized once), **Unified mechanic** (the engine operation + grammar impact), and **Danger notes** (rules machinery that breaks or gets interesting when constraints are lifted). Citations are dotted CR rules; every one was retrieved and read during this analysis. "Empty cell" never means "illegal in principle" — it means *no rule or card currently instantiates it*, which is exactly the design space an engine should leave reachable.

**Maintenance.** After CR refreshes, beyond running `scripts/cite check`, re-examine each family's "empty cells" — a newly filled cell (a new set printing one of the missing instances) is a finding to record, not an error; update the family and the engine directives accordingly.

## 1. The cast/play zone-permission matrix

**Shape.** `permit(player P, action ∈ {cast, play-land}, object O, source-zone Z, zone-owner W, window T, cost-mode C)` — permission to begin casting/playing an object out of a zone.

**Instances.** The defaults are stated zone-generically and then immediately constrained: "To cast a spell is to take it from where it is (usually the hand), put it on the stack, and pay its costs" (601.2); "To play a land means to put it onto the battlefield *from the zone it's in* (usually the hand)" (701.18a). The actual default permissions are per-card-type rules hardwired to ⟨hand, self⟩: 301.1, 302.1, 303.1, 304.1, 306.1, 307.1, 310.1 ("A player who has priority may cast a … card *from their hand*…"), 305.1 for lands. Everything else is an effect-granted exception: graveyard (flashback 702.34a, escape 702.138a), exile (madness 702.35a, plot 702.170d, suspend 702.62a, cipher's encoded copy 702.99a, foretell per 702.143a), library top (601.3e's examples: Garruk's Horde, Melek), face-down exile gated on look-permission (601.3f), command zone (commander, 903.8; Attractions enter play from a command-zone deck, 701.51b), and Crucible of Worlds' "You may play lands from your graveyard" (oracle text verified). The gate is uniform: "A player can begin to cast a spell only if a rule or effect allows that player to cast it and no rule or effect prohibits" (601.3).

**Asymmetry.** The permission matrix is filled cell-by-cell by individual keywords instead of by one parameterized rule, and two cells are structurally empty: **the stack** and **another player's hidden zones directly** (cross-player casting is always laundered through exile or copy). Yet the CR already proves stack-adjacency: 707.12 creates a copy "*in the same zone the object is in*" and casts it "while another spell or ability is resolving" — a zone-generic, mid-resolution cast that works from hand, graveyard, exile, and in principle the stack itself. 608.2g (casting during resolution) and 723.5's "controlling another player" (choices over another player's objects) fill in the other coordinates separately. Precedent of prior generalization: "play" was retro-unified over "cast" (701.18b, 701.18d–e errata history), and 601.3e/601.3f were added precisely to make non-hand, non-face-up sources legality-checkable.

**Unified mechanic.** Model one operation `begin_cast(object, from=(zone, owner), via=permission-source)` whose legality is the 601.3 conjunction over a *permission set* — entries carrying (zone, owner, object-predicate, timing-window, cost-mode). The per-type rules 301.1–310.1 become seven standing permission entries, keywords like flashback become card-granted entries, and 707.12 becomes `begin_cast(copy_of(object), from=object.zone)`. Grammar: the productions already exist — `you may cast ~ from your graveyard`, `cast … from among face-down cards in exile`, `play lands from your graveyard` are attested; the grammar needs only a generalized `from ⟨zone descriptor⟩` complement on `cast/play`, with owner as an optional modifier (`from target opponent's graveyard` already parses under the same production).

**Danger notes.** Casting from the stack: 601.2a moves the card to the stack — a no-op move that 400.7 identity rules never contemplated (does it become a new object? 707.12 dodges this by casting a *copy*, strongly suggesting the engine should too). Casting from another player's library/hand collides with hidden-zone visibility (400.2 via `state.md` §3) — 601.3f already shows the required pattern: permission must be coupled with look-permission. And 733.1's rewind carve-out (actions touching libraries are irreversible) means any library-sourced cast needs its rewind boundary precomputed.

## 2. Library-operation verbs: owner and position are frozen parameters

**Shape.** `library_op(actor A, library-owner W, position ∈ {top, bottom}, count N, destinations D, look/reveal mode)` — every one of draw/mill/scry/surveil/fateseal/explore is this one operation with different bindings.

**Instances.** Draw: "putting the top card of *their* library into their hand" (121.1). Mill: "puts that many cards from the top of *their* library into their graveyard" (701.17a) — owner-parameterized via the verb's subject ("target player mills"). Scry: "look at the top N cards of *your* library, then … bottom … top" (701.22a). **Fateseal: "look at the top N cards of *an opponent's* library, then put any number of them on the bottom of *that* library…" (701.29a)** — scry with the owner parameter flipped. Surveil: scry with destination=graveyard, owner locked to "your" (701.25a). Explore: "that permanent's controller reveals the top card of *their* library…" (701.44a). Search is already fully general: "To search for a card in a zone, look at all cards in that zone (even if it's a hidden zone)" (701.23a), and routinely runs on other players' libraries (701.23b example: "Search its controller's graveyard, hand, and library…").

**Asymmetry.** The grid {verb} × {whose library} is almost empty off the diagonal: draw, scry, surveil, explore are hardwired to the actor's own library; only fateseal (owner=opponent) and mill (owner=subject) escape. There is no "draw from target opponent's library," no "surveil an opponent." Fateseal is the smoking-gun precedent: WotC built it by copying 701.22a and editing one word, demonstrating the CR tolerates owner-generalization of top-of-library verbs with zero new machinery. The position parameter is similarly frozen: everything reads the *top*; bottom-of-library interactions exist only as destinations (701.22a, 701.29a).

**Unified mechanic.** One engine primitive: `inspect_and_route(actor, library(owner), top_n, routing)` where routing maps each looked-at card to (zone, owner, position, face). Draw = route(top 1 → owner's hand); scry = partition(top N → top|bottom of same library); surveil = partition(top N → graveyard|top); fateseal = scry with owner≠actor; explore = reveal(top 1) + conditional routing. Trigger events ("whenever a player scries/surveils", 701.22d, 701.25d) become predicates over the parameter tuple, not per-verb hooks. Grammar: the verbs are lexical keyword actions (701), so generalization is free at the grammar level — `fateseal` already shows the surface form needs no owner argument because the verb's定义 binds it; a generalized form would surface as ordinary English ("target opponent surveils 2", already grammatical under existing subject-verb productions).

**Danger notes.** Draw is special-cased everywhere: empty-library loss is an SBA on the *drawer* (121.4, 704.5b) — if A draws from B's library, who loses? Draw-replacement effects (121.6, 614.11-family: "applied even if no cards could be drawn", 121.6a) key on the *player who draws*, not the library touched; the engine must keep those two roles distinct **now** (`drawer` vs `source_library_owner`) even while they're always equal. Hidden-zone knowledge: drawing from an opponent's library transfers information 400-series visibility rules never priced in; mill avoids this by routing to a public zone. Also note 121.5: moving library→hand *without the word "draw"* is already a different event — the engine's event vocabulary must distinguish the verb from the movement, which is precisely what makes the generalization safe.

## 3. The timing-permission lattice — and the missing triggered-ability column

**Shape.** `timing(action-kind ∈ {cast, activate, special-action, trigger-resolution}, window-predicate over (priority, stack-state, phase/step, whose-turn, combat-state), polarity ∈ {permit, restrict, forbid})`.

**Instances.** Defaults: instants any time with priority, noninstants at sorcery speed (117.1a); activated abilities any time (117.1b); special actions split between the two regimes (117.1c). Restriction-side: "only as a sorcery"/"any time they could cast a sorcery" is a defined timing idiom (307.5); "Activate only as a sorcery / as an instant" (602.5d, 602.5e); loyalty abilities: sorcery-speed plus once-per-permanent-per-turn (606.3); forecast: "only during the upkeep step of the card's owner and only once each turn," activated from hand (702.57a–b); unlock costs: main phase, empty stack (709.5e); equip/fortify/level-up bake "Activate only as a sorcery" into the keyword (702.6a, 702.67a, 702.87a). Permission-side: flash (702.8a), "as though it had flash" with quality- and cost-conditioned variants (601.3b–d), conditional flash surviving proposal (601.6a via `casting.md`, verified there against 601.6). Window-predicates richer than sorcery/instant are attested in oracle text: "Cast this spell only during combat after blockers are declared," "…only during combat on an opponent's turn" (corpus-verified). Forbid-side: split second (702.61a), Void Winnower-style prohibitions handled by 601.3a.

**Asymmetry.** Casts and activations have a full three-polarity lattice (permit / restrict / forbid, each parameterized by window-predicates). **Triggered abilities have none of it**: once triggered, the ability goes on the stack "the next time a player would receive priority" (603.3) — mandatory, immediate, no timing knob. There is no "this triggers, but resolves only at sorcery speed," no "this ability triggers only during combat" as a *timing permission* (trigger conditions can mention phases, but that's the event filter, not a resolution window). The CR already emulates the missing instance constantly: delayed triggered abilities deliberately park an effect at a chosen future window ("At the beginning of the next end step…", 603.7a) — that is a trigger with a hand-rolled timing restriction. Precedent for the lattice being extensible: 602.5e ("Activate only as an instant") exists purely for symmetry, and 601.3b–d show the permit side acquiring ever-finer condition parameters.

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: every "begin/perform action" event carries a `window` predicate evaluated against (priority-holder, stack, phase/step, turn-owner, combat sub-state); defaults from 117.1a–c are just the standing predicates; keywords contribute predicate rewrites (flash: weaken to instant-window; "only as a sorcery": strengthen). Triggered abilities get the same field: today it is the constant "next priority" (603.3); a generalized trigger would set `stack_entry_window` (when it may/must go on the stack) or `resolution_window`. Delayed triggers (603.7) then become sugar: trigger-now-resolve-later collapses into a window on one ability. Grammar: activation instructions after the colon already parse as a separate slot (602.1b via `grammar.md` §2); a triggered counterpart needs one new production — a trailing instruction sentence on a triggered ability ("Resolve this ability only as a sorcery"), structurally identical to the activated one, so the grammar cost is near zero.

**Danger notes.** A sorcery-speed trigger must wait on the stack or wait *off* the stack; each choice breaks something. Waiting off-stack interacts with 603.3b's APNAP batching and with state triggers (603.8 re-trigger suppression assumes prompt stacking). Waiting on-stack but "can't resolve yet" violates 608.1's pass-in-succession resolution contract and lets players stall the stack. The clean model is the one the CR already chose: fire now, *create a delayed trigger for the window* (603.7a) — i.e., the engine should implement timing-restricted triggers as auto-generated delayed triggers, which also answers what split second does to them (702.61b: triggers still trigger and stack normally under split second — the forbid-polarity never touched triggers, confirming they live outside the lattice today).

## 4. Progress tracks: five state representations for one monotonic counter-with-thresholds

**Shape.** `track(carrier, value, advance-rule, threshold → ability map, terminal-rule)` — a monotonically advancing value whose thresholds enable or fire abilities, with an optional end-of-track cleanup.

**Instances.** (1) **Sagas**: lore counters; auto-advance each precombat main (714.3c, turn-based action 703.4f); thresholds are chapter triggers "if the number of lore counters … was less than N and became at least N" (714.2b); terminal rule: sacrifice SBA at final chapter (714.4). (2) **Levelers**: level counters via "Level up [cost]: … Activate only as a sorcery" (702.87a); thresholds are static bands "as long as … at least N1 … no more than N2" (711.2a–b); no terminal. (3) **Classes**: level is a *designation*, not counters — "[Cost]: This Class's level becomes N. Activate only if this Class is level N-1 and only as a sorcery" (716.2a); "A level is a designation that any permanent can have" (716.2b); default level 1 (716.2d). (4) **Dungeons**: a venture *marker position* on a command-zone card (309.4, 309.4a); advance = "venture" choosing an arrow (701.49b); rooms are triggered abilities (309.4c); terminal: remove-from-game SBA (704.5t). (5) **The Ring**: a per-player *temptation count* unlocking cumulative abilities on an emblem at 2+/3+/4+ (701.54c). (6) **Speed**: a per-player integer 1–4 (702.179b, 702.179e), advanced by an inherent once-per-turn trigger (702.179d), initialized by an SBA (702.179a). (7) Attractions' lit-light visits are the stochastic cousin (701.52a, 717.2).

**Asymmetry.** One shape, five disjoint state mechanisms: counters-on-permanent, designation, marker-position, per-player count, per-player scalar. The CR even *forbids* cross-talk it had to notice: "class levels do not interact with level counters" (702.87c) — an explicit firewall between two implementations of the same idea. Counters get proliferate-support (701.34a); designations, markers, and counts don't (you cannot proliferate a Class level, a venture marker, or the Ring's count — only Saga/leveler counters). Advance-rules are similarly scattered: turn-based action (Saga), activated ability (leveler/Class), keyword action (venture), inherent trigger (speed). Precedent that representation can migrate: speed's "no speed counts as 0" (702.179f) and Class's "no level = level 1" (716.2d) are the same defaulting rule invented twice.

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: one `ProgressTrack {carrier: object|player, value: int, advance: Event→Δ, thresholds: [(predicate, ability/trigger, fire-mode ∈ {edge (714.2b), level (711.2a)})], terminal: SBA}`. The two fire-modes are exactly the Saga-vs-leveler distinction: chapter abilities are *edge-triggered* on crossing (714.2b), bands are *level-sensitive* statics (711.2a–b) — capture both and every current track plus future ones (and "read ahead"'s choose-your-entry-point, 714.3b) is a parameterization. Whether the value is exposed as counters (proliferatable) becomes a flag, not an architecture. Grammar: the striated-box band parsers (`{rN} —`, `LEVEL N-M`, class level bars; `grammar.md` §1–2) already emit threshold structures; a unified track means all three band grammars compile to one AST node.

**Danger notes.** Edge-triggering needs the *transition*, not the state — 714.2b's "was less than N and became at least N" forces the engine to evaluate thresholds against (old, new) pairs, including multi-counter jumps firing several chapters at once. Terminal SBAs are guarded by "isn't the source of a … ability that has triggered but not yet left the stack" (714.4, 704.5t) — the unified terminal rule must carry that pending-trigger guard. If tracks become proliferatable uniformly, dungeons and the Ring suddenly interact with counter-doublers (614.16) — a deliberate design lever, but one that today's firewall (702.87c) exists to prevent; the engine should make the firewall a per-track flag rather than a type distinction.

## 5. The attachment/host relation

**Shape.** `attach(attachment A, host H ∈ objects ∪ players, legality-predicate L(A,H), violation-policy V)` plus the broader notion of *composite objects* (attachment's limiting case: occupying the same object).

**Instances.** The CR already states the general operation: "To attach an Aura, Equipment, or Fortification *to an object or player* means to take it from where it currently is and put it onto that object or player … can't be attached to an object or player it couldn't enchant, equip, or fortify" (701.3a), with a uniform failure semantics (701.3b) and re-timestamping (701.3c). The three subtypes are the same relation with different L and V: Auras — L = enchant keyword, hosts include players (303.4, 702.5a, 702.5d: "Auras that can enchant a player can target and be attached to players"), V = graveyard SBA (704.5m); Equipment — L = creature-only (301.5), V = unattach-and-stay SBA (704.5n); Fortification — L = land-only (301.6), V = same. Catch-all V for everything else: 704.5p. Host-class extensions already shipped: "Equip planeswalker … as though that planeswalker were a creature" (702.6e). Adjacent composite relations: cipher's "encoded" — an *exiled card* bound to a creature, surviving control changes (702.99a–c); mutate/merge — components stacked into one object (702.140c, 730.2, 730.2a); melded pairs (701.42a); Room halves as co-resident sub-objects (709.5); Role-token uniqueness policed per-controller by SBA (704.5y); Adventures/prepare as alternative-identity sub-objects (715.2, 722.3).

**Asymmetry.** L is hardwired per subtype rather than per card: Auras may host on players, Equipment may not (301.5: "can't legally be attached to anything that isn't a creature") — there is no "Equip player" even though "curse-Equipment" is the obvious missing cell and 701.3a's signature already accepts players. Battles are barred from being attachments entirely (310.9). V (what happens on illegal attachment) comes in exactly two flavors — die (704.5m) vs. unattach (704.5n/p) — assigned by subtype, not by card. Precedents: 702.6e (equip planeswalker) lifted the host class once; curses lifted Aura hosts to players; cipher attached a card *from exile*, proving the relation doesn't even require both ends on the battlefield.

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: one `attached_to` edge on any object, with per-attachment `host_predicate` (an object/player predicate compiled from `Enchant X` / `Equip [quality]` / `Fortify` text) and `violation_policy ∈ {to_graveyard, unattach}`; the three SBAs 704.5m/n/p become one parameterized SBA. Merged permanents (730.2) are the degenerate attachment where the host relation is "same object, ordered components" — keep components as a list (730.3 needs them individually on exit, per `state.md` §7). Grammar: `Enchant ⟨descriptor⟩` and `Equip ⟨quality⟩` already parse as keyword+parameter (`grammar.md` §3); a unified relation needs no new production — only the catalogs stop being three closed host-classes.

**Danger notes.** Protection interacts per-subtype: "can't be enchanted by" (702.16c) vs. "can't be equipped by" (702.16d) — a unified attach must decompose protection's E into a predicate over the *attachment relation*, not the subtype. Control/grant asymmetries: 303.4e/301.5d give activation rights of granted abilities to the *host's* controller — for player-hosts, "the enchanted player" already works (303.4e), but Equipment-on-player has no analog of "equipped creature" wording (301.5f's referent machinery would need a player branch). Re-timestamping on attach (701.3c, 613.7e) means generalized attachment churn has layer-system consequences. And battles: 310.9's prohibition exists because a battle carries a *protector* designation (310.8) whose combat semantics (310.8b–d) would tangle with a host's.

## 6. The targeting/interaction-permission ladder

**Shape.** `guard(carrier ∈ {permanent, player}, interaction ∈ {target, enchant/equip/attach, block, damage, …}, source-predicate S, response ∈ {forbid, toll(cost), counter-unless})`.

**Instances.** Shroud: forbid targeting, S = everyone — "This permanent *or player* can't be the target of spells or abilities" (702.18a). Hexproof: forbid targeting, S = opponents (702.11b–c, player form explicit). Hexproof-from-[quality]: S = opponents ∧ quality (702.11d), with shorthand expansion semantics (702.11f–g). Ward: *toll* instead of forbid — "Whenever this permanent becomes the target of a spell or ability an opponent controls, counter that spell or ability unless that player pays [cost]" (702.21a). Protection: the same guard fanned across four interactions at once — targeting (702.16b), attachment (702.16c–d), blocking (702.16f), damage (prevention, 702.16e) — with S as an arbitrary "quality … any characteristic value or information" (702.16a), including S = a specific player (702.16k) and S = everything (702.16j). Evasion abilities are the same guard on the block interaction alone: a restriction "created by an evasion ability," cumulative (509.1b).

**Asymmetry.** The matrix {interaction} × {S} × {response} × {carrier} is sparsely and irregularly filled. Ward exists only with response=toll and only on permanents — corpus-verified: every "have ward" grant targets permanents ("Artifacts you control have ward {1}", "Commanders you control have ward {2}"); no "you have ward" exists, while shroud/hexproof/protection all have player forms (702.18a, 702.11c, 702.16b). There is no toll-variant of protection ("attach to this only if you pay…"), no hexproof-from for blocking, no ward against *being attacked*. Precedents of prior generalization within the family: hexproof → hexproof-from (702.11d) parameterized S; protection from [quality] → from a player (702.16k) → from everything (702.16j) parameterized S twice more; ward itself is the precedent that *response* is a parameter (forbid → toll).

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: a guard list per carrier, each entry `(interaction-kind, source-predicate, response)`; shroud/hexproof/protection/ward/evasion become library entries. Targeting legality (601.2c) then consults one mechanism; the SBA cleanups protection causes (702.16c–d) are the attachment violation-policy from Family 5 firing off the same predicate. Grammar: `hexproof from ⟨quality⟩`, `protection from ⟨quality⟩ and from ⟨quality⟩`, `ward ⟨cost⟩` all parse today as keyword+parameter (`grammar.md` §3, including the multi-stem guard); the general form needs one production for response-toll on arbitrary interactions ("…can't be blocked unless its controller pays {2}" — already near-attested shapes in evasion text).

**Danger notes.** Ward is a *triggered* ability (702.21a) while the rest are static — unifying them means representing forbid as a static legality predicate but toll as a generated trigger; the engine should compile both from the same guard record rather than pretending they're one runtime kind. Protection's damage leg is a prevention effect with all of 615's shield bookkeeping (615.1, 615.7) and the "can't be prevented" interaction (615 family) — response=prevent is a third response type, not a forbid. And 702.11e's lose-hexproof-implies-lose-variants rule shows the CR wants subtype lattices ("X-from is an X") — the engine's guard taxonomy needs subsumption, not flat names.

## 7. Counters: the carrier domain is general, the verb domain is not

**Shape.** `counter_op(verb ∈ {place, remove, move, proliferate-style copy}, kind K, carrier ∈ objects ∪ players, count N)`.

**Instances.** The carrier domain is maximally general by definition: "A counter is a marker placed on an *object or player*" (122.1), and objects include spells, cards in any zone, abilities on the stack (109.1). Non-battlefield carriers are explicitly live: +X/+Y counters work "on a creature card in a zone other than the battlefield" (122.1a); keyword counters likewise (122.1b); suspend keeps time counters on exiled cards and triggers on their removal (702.62a–b); Skullbriar's "Counters remain on Skullbriar as it moves to any zone other than a player's hand or library" (oracle-verified) puts +1/+1 counters on a *spell on the stack*. Player counters: poison/rad (122.1f-adjacent, 122.1i), energy/ticket (107.14, 107.17a via `state.md`). Default persistence: counters cease on zone change (122.2). SBA maintenance: annihilation (704.5q) and enforced maxima (704.5r). Each counter carries a timestamp (613.7c).

**Asymmetry.** The *verbs* are narrower than the carrier domain: proliferate reaches only "permanents and/or players that have a counter" (701.34a) — a suspended card in exile, a counter-laden spell on the stack, all invisible to it; bolster only "a creature you control" (701.39a); support only creatures (701.41a); adapt only self (701.46a). The replacement-effect doubler hook is likewise scoped: "would put one or more counters *on a permanent*" (614.16). So the rules can *hold* counters almost anywhere but can only *manipulate* them fluently on the battlefield and on players. Precedent: the carrier domain itself is the generalization — 122.1's "object or player" was already the lift from permanents-only, and the kind-system absorbed keyword counters (122.1b) without new machinery.

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: `place_counters(kind, carrier: object|player, n)` as the single primitive with carrier unrestricted (the data model in `engine.md` already gives both Permanent and Player a `counters` map — extend to all objects); verbs are thin wrappers with *selector predicates* (proliferate = "each chosen carrier with ≥1 counter gets one of each kind it has"); doubler replacements (614.16) key on the event, with the current permanent-scope as a predicate, not a type. Grammar: no new productions — "put a +1/+1 counter on ⟨referent⟩" already takes arbitrary referents; the only grammar-visible asymmetry is in keyword-action definitions, which are lexical.

**Danger notes.** Counters off the battlefield are mostly *inert by rule*: 122.1a–b enumerate exactly which kinds function elsewhere; a generalized verb domain needs a per-kind "functions in zone Z" mask or you get loyalty counters doing things on graveyard cards. 122.2's cease-on-zone-change is the default the suspend/Skullbriar exceptions override — model persistence as a policy field per (kind, effect), or LKI questions multiply. Proliferate over hidden zones is the real landmine: choosing "any number of … that have a counter" requires knowing what has counters — fine for exile (public, 400.2), incoherent for hands/libraries; expansion should stop at public zones, exactly the line 701.17c draws for milled-card references.

## 8. Copy semantics: one pipeline, three entry points, one persistence wall (already breached)

**Shape.** `copy(source O, mode ∈ {snapshot-object, stack-copy, cast-copy, become-copy}, destination (zone, position), lifetime-policy)`.

**Instances.** The umbrella is stated up front: "Some objects become or turn another object into a 'copy' of a spell, permanent, or card. Some effects create a token that's a copy of another object" (707.1). Copiable values and the choices-snapshot for stack objects (707.2). Stack-copy: "put a copy of it onto the stack; a copy of a spell isn't cast" — inherits modes/targets/X/costs (707.10), with optional retargeting (707.10c). Cast-copy: copy created "in the same zone the object is in" then run through 601.2a–h (707.12); cipher casts copies of an encoded exiled card (702.99a). Become-copy: layer-1 continuous effects (613.1a, 707.9 modifications); even a *permanent becoming a copy of a spell* exists ("…up to one other target permanent you control becomes a copy of that spell until end of turn", The Myriad Pools, corpus-verified). Lifetime wall: "If a copy of a spell is in a zone other than the stack, it ceases to exist. If a copy of a card is in any zone other than the stack or the battlefield, it ceases to exist" (704.5e); tokens likewise (704.5d). **The wall has already been breached on purpose**: prepare creates a copy *in exile* that persists — "This is an exception to rule 704.5e. For as long as the copy remains in exile, the prepared permanent's controller may cast the copy" (722.3c).

**Asymmetry.** Entry points are unevenly available: you can copy spells and activated/triggered abilities on the stack (707.10) but not static abilities (they're never objects, 113.9-adjacent); you can cast a copy from any zone (707.12) but *create a persistent copy-object* in a zone only via the one bespoke exception (722.3c); copies inherit choices in mode 707.10 but make fresh choices in mode 707.12 — a parameter (choice-inheritance) currently welded to the entry point. Precedents: 722.3c (persistence lifted), 715.3c (copies inherit Adventure-ness), 707.8a (double-faced copy tokens got a whole sub-rule), 112.2a (owner rules patched for created-and-cast copies) — the copy system has been parameter-patched four times, each time ad hoc.

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: one `make_copy(source, dest_zone, lifetime, choice_policy ∈ {inherit, fresh}, then ∈ {nothing, cast, attach…})`. 707.10 = (stack, until-leaves-stack, inherit, nothing); 707.12 = (source.zone, transient, fresh, cast); 722.3c = (exile, while-host-prepared, fresh-at-cast, none); token-copy = (battlefield, token-rules, n/a). The SBAs 704.5d–e become the default lifetime policy, overridable per effect exactly as 722.3c already does. Grammar: "cast a copy of ⟨object⟩," "copy target spell," "becomes a copy of ⟨object⟩" are distinct attested productions mapping cleanly onto `mode`; nothing new needed until persistent-copy effects start naming destinations ("create a copy of ⟨card⟩ in exile" — one production).

**Danger notes.** Choice-inheritance is the subtle parameter: 707.10 copies even cost-payment facts (its Fling example reads the original's sacrificed creature), while 707.12 re-runs 601.2 — mixing these wrongly produces nonsense like a cast-copy that thinks costs were paid. Copies of permanent spells become tokens on resolution (608.3f via `casting.md`) — lifetime policy must hand off between "spell copy" and "token" regimes mid-flight. And copying *across* the face-down boundary is already special (708.2's listed characteristics are copiable; 707.2's Grinning Demon example) — a generalized copier must always copy through the layer-1/copiable-value lens, never raw card text.

## 9. Decision-authority delegation: from one combat requirement to a whole turn

**Shape.** `delegate(decision-scope, from-player, to-player|constraint, duration)` — the game reassigning or constraining who makes a class of choices.

**Instances.** Smallest scope, constraint-flavored: goad — "attacks each combat if able and attacks a player other than [the goader] if able" (701.15b), stacking per-player (701.15c), implemented through the generic requirement/restriction solver at declare-attackers (508.1c–d: maximize requirements obeyed without violating restrictions). Single-choice reassignment: "you may choose new targets for the copy" (707.10c); battle protectors re-rooting "defending player" decisions to a non-controller (310.8a–d: protector blocks for the battle, 310.8c). Object-control transfer: layer-2 control effects (613.1b), exchange control (701.12b), spell controller reassignment implied by 112.2's "by default." Whole-player control: section 723 — "makes all choices and decisions the controlled player is allowed to make" (723.5), turn-scoped (723.1) or limited-duration (723.2), with the crucial invariant "Only control of the player changes. All objects are controlled by their normal controllers" (723.3).

**Asymmetry.** The spectrum has occupied endpoints (one combat requirement; an entire player) and a nearly empty middle: there is no "an opponent chooses this ability's targets" as a standing mechanic, no delegation of blocking declarations alone (the protector rules 310.8c–d come closest, and exist only for battles), no per-decision-kind control ("you make X's discard choices"). Precedents: protectors (310.8) re-bound the *defending player* role away from controller — a one-decision-class delegation invented for battles; goad showed constraint-style delegation composes via the existing 508.1d solver; 723 proved total delegation is formalizable.

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: route every player choice through a `decide(decision-kind, nominal-decider) → actual-decider + constraints` indirection. Goad contributes constraints; protector rewrites the decider for battle-defense decisions (310.8d says exactly this: "all rules and effects that refer to the 'defending player' … refer to that battle's protector"); 723 rewrites the decider for *all* kinds. This is cheap insurance: the engine likely already centralizes choice-prompting for UI reasons; making the decider resolvable per decision-kind is a field, not a redesign. Grammar: goad-style text compiles to requirement/restriction records (existing productions); explicit delegation text ("target opponent chooses …") already parses as ordinary subject-rebinding — `grammar.md` §5 notes the grammar only captures *who chooses*.

**Danger notes.** 723.3's invariant is what keeps total delegation sound — object control and player control are different axes; an engine that conflates "controller of the player's decisions" with "controller of their objects" breaks Mindslaver immediately. Hidden information: 723.5-style control implies seeing the controlled player's hand — partial delegations need explicit visibility grants or they leak/starve information. Requirement-stacking (701.15c vs 701.15d: re-goading by the *same* player adds nothing) means constraints must be keyed by (source-player, constraint) pairs, deduplicated — a set, not a multiset.

## 10. Combat-role eligibility: three predicates the rules edit one clause at a time

**Shape.** `combat_role(role ∈ {attacker, attack-target, blocker, defense-decider}, eligibility-predicate)`.

**Instances.** Attacker: "creatures that they control … untapped, they can't also be battles, and each one must either have haste or have been controlled … since the turn began" (508.1a). Attack-target: "which player, planeswalker, or battle each of the chosen creatures is attacking" (508.1b); battles attackable by rule (310.5), with per-battle eligibility derived from the protector ("can be attacked by any attacking player for whom its protector is a defending player," 310.8b — including its own controller for Sieges). Blocker: untapped creatures, not battles, blocking attackers of their controller's head or protected battle (509.1a, 310.8c). Damage recipients as the superset: "battles, creatures, planeswalkers, and players" (120.1), nothing else (120.1a). Non-creatures buy their way in by becoming creatures: crew — "This permanent becomes an artifact creature until end of turn" (702.122a).

**Asymmetry.** The attack-target set has been generalized **twice** (planeswalkers, then battles — 508.1b's list and 310.5 are the receipts), and "any target" (115.4) was added as the matching grammar shorthand; the *attacker* set has never been generalized — only creatures attack, and the rules even had to add "can't also be battles" patches to 508.1a/509.1a when battles arrived, showing the predicates are maintained by hand. Blockers similarly: creatures only. There is no "this planeswalker can block" or "this battle attacks" even though the declare-attackers machinery (508.1c–d requirements/restrictions) is already entity-agnostic. Precedent beyond the double generalization: vehicles/crew institutionalized the "temporarily satisfy the attacker predicate" workaround (702.122a).

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: store the three eligibility predicates as data — `can_attack(obj)`, `attackable(entity)`, `can_block(obj)` — seeded from 508.1a/b, 509.1a, and consulted by the declaration legality checker; the requirement/restriction solver (508.1c–d, 509.1b–c) stays untouched because it already operates on declared sets, not types. Damage recipients (120.1) are a fourth predicate the first three must stay subsets of. Grammar: "X can attack as though it were a creature" needs no new production shape — it's the 702.6e "as though that planeswalker were a creature" pattern, already attested in keyword text.

**Danger notes.** Attacker-eligibility leaks into power/toughness machinery: combat damage assignment reads power (510-series via `actions.md` 703.4k) — a battle or planeswalker attacking needs a damage stat (battles have defense, not power; 310.4). The protector web (310.8b–d) shows every new attackable type may need a *defense-decider* rule; the engine should make "who defends this entity" a stored reference (players: self; planeswalkers: controller; battles: protector) instead of re-deriving it. And summoning sickness (302.6/508.1a's continuous-control clause) is phrased for creatures — a generalized attacker predicate must decide whether the clause is part of the role or of the type.

## 11. Replacement, prevention, redirection, skips, doublers: one event-rewriter, five marker dialects

**Shape.** `rewrite(event-pattern, transform, consumption-policy, chooser)` applied in the 616.1 fixpoint.

**Instances.** The base kind with its marker taxonomy: "instead" (614.1a), "skip" (614.1b = replace-with-nothing, 614.10), ETB modifiers (614.1c–d, e.g. Saga lore counters 714.3a, battle defense counters 310.4b), turned-face-up modifiers (614.1e). Specializations that are *defined as* replacement effects: redirection — "replace damage dealt to one battle, creature, planeswalker, or player with the same damage dealt to another" (614.9); skips with their counting semantics (614.10a); draw-replacements with empty-library applicability (121.6, 121.6a–c); token/counter doublers chaining onto other replacements (614.16). Prevention is the same watcher with a quantity-consumption policy: "watch for a damage event … act like 'shields'" (615.1), shields decrement per point (615.7), whole-instance variants (615.8 via `effects.md`), and the modified event "never happens" in both systems (614.6/615.6). Ordering and choosing are already unified: 616.1 runs replacement *and/or* prevention effects through one chooser sequence (616.1a–g).

**Asymmetry.** Prevention's shield machinery — quantified, consumable, source-checked — exists **only for damage** (615.1's "watch for a damage event"). There is no shield grammar for life loss, counter placement, or card draws; those get ad-hoc one-shot replacements instead ("If you would lose life, …instead"), losing the N-point bookkeeping (615.7) and the "can't be prevented" interaction (615.12 via `effects.md`). Redirection likewise exists only for damage (614.9). Skips exist only for events/steps/phases/turns (614.10). Precedent: the CR already treats prevention as a sub-dialect — 616.1 makes no distinction when ordering, and shield counters define one replacement + one prevention effect in a single breath (122.1c) — i.e., the unification is half-done in the rules themselves.

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: the `effects.md` §9 fixpoint is the right runtime; the upgrade is making `transform` a typed algebra — `substitute(event')` (614.1a), `null` (skip), `retarget(recipient)` (614.9), `scale/augment` (614.16, doublers), `decrement-shield(N)` (615.7) — with `consumption ∈ {once-per-event (614.5), N-points, whole-instance}` and `event-pattern` over the whole event vocabulary, damage being just one event type. Then "prevent the next 3 life loss" is a new parameter binding, not new machinery. Grammar: §4 of `grammar.md` already tags the marker dialects; the unified algebra means the parser emits one node type with a transform field — and the known hazard (one-shot "skip" vs continuous "skip", documented there) stays a tagging rule, not a semantic split.

**Danger notes.** "Can't be prevented" (615.12) and unpreventable-analog interactions don't exist for non-damage shields — generalizing shields means deciding whether "can't" effects (614.17) get a matching override lattice per event type. Redirection's legality recheck at apply-time (614.9's "no longer a battle, creature, or planeswalker … does nothing") generalizes to "retarget target still matches pattern" — cheap if retarget is typed, a bug factory if not. The 616.1b–d priority steps are *event-specific* (control-of-entry, copy-of-entry, back-face-entry) — a generalized rewriter needs that priority list to be data, since new event types will eventually demand their own must-choose-first categories.

## 12. Designations and inherent triggers: the flag system the CR keeps reinventing

**Shape.** `flag(carrier ∈ {player, permanent, card, game}, multiplicity, gain-rule, loss/persistence-policy, payload ∈ {marker-only, inherent-abilities, value})`.

**Instances** (each verified): monarch — single-holder player flag with two source-less inherent triggers (725.2, "no source … exception to rule 113.8"); initiative — same shape, three triggers (726.2); city's blessing — multi-holder, never lost (702.131a–c via `designations.md`); day/night — game-carried tri-state (731.1 via `designations.md`); Ring-bearer — per-player permanent designation + emblem + count (701.54a–c); goaded — permanent flag with duration and per-goader set (701.15a–d); suspected (701.60 via `designations.md`); unlocked door halves (709.5c–g); Class level — value-typed designation "any permanent can have" (716.2b); sector — enum designation with SBA assignment (702.158b–c, 704.5u); protector — battle-carried *player reference* (310.8, 310.8f single-holder per battle); prepared — flag with an attached exile-copy payload (722.3a–c); saddled, solved, monstrous, renowned, harnessed (designations.md, anchored at 701.37b's "no rules meaning other than to act as a marker"); commander as a zone-independent *card attribute* (903.3 via `designations.md`); speed as a value-typed player flag with an inherent trigger (702.179b–d); rad counters carrying an inherent trigger on a *counter* (122.1i).

**Asymmetry.** Every column of the flag schema is instantiated *somewhere*, but no flag gets the whole schema: monarch transfers on combat damage (725.2) but city's blessing can never be lost (702.131a); goaded stacks per-source (701.15c) but suspected can't double (701.60d); Class level is a value, sector an enum, most others booleans; only monarch/initiative/speed/rad carry inherent triggered abilities; only prepared carries an object payload. Nothing lets a *card* gain a designation mid-game except commander-adjacent attributes (903.3). Precedents that the schema generalizes: 716.2b's "a level is a designation that *any permanent* can have" decoupled a flag from its mechanic; the inherent-trigger pattern was reused verbatim across 725.2 → 726.2 → 702.179d → 122.1i, i.e., it's already a template.

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: one `Flag` record — `(name, carrier-class, multiplicity ∈ {unique-in-game, unique-per-carrier-group, unbounded}, gain, loss, copiable?: bool (almost always no: 701.15b, 701.54b, 702.158b, 716.2b), inherent_abilities: [trigger/static], payload)`. Single-holder semantics (monarch 725.3-style steal-on-gain) and succession-on-leave (725.4) become multiplicity policies. The `designations.md` table is the seed data; the *next step* the seam asks for is making gain/loss verbs generic ("X becomes/stops being [flag]") so future designations are content, not code. Grammar: designation verbs are already isolated lexical patterns ("becomes the monarch," "is goaded," "becomes prepared" — corpus-attested); one production family `⟨subject⟩ becomes/is/stops being ⟨designation⟩` covers them.

**Danger notes.** Source-less inherent triggers break the ability-source model (113.8 exception, stated in 725.2/726.2) — the engine's trigger records need a nullable source *now*. Flags interleave with continuous-effect timing: ascend re-applies continuous effects before trigger checks (702.131d via `designations.md`) — gaining a flag is a state change that must participate in the SBA/trigger fixpoint, not a side annotation. Copiability is uniformly "no" today, but 709.5's unlocked-designation statics are *part of copiable values* (709.5 text) — the boundary is per-flag, so the `copiable?` field is not optional.

## 13. Mana attributes: color is a type, everything else is a rider

**Shape.** `mana_unit(type, source-snapshot, spend-predicate, on-spend-effects, persistence)`.

**Instances.** The closed type system: "There are six types of mana: white, blue, black, red, green, and colorless" (106.1b). Source tracking: "If mana is produced by a spell, the source of that mana is that spell" (106.3). Spend-restrictions, riders, and delayed triggers piggyback without changing type: "restrict how that mana can be spent, have an additional effect … or create a delayed triggered ability … that triggers when that mana is spent. This doesn't affect the mana's type" (106.6), with per-mana effect replication under doublers (106.6a). Snow is *not* a type but a source-property predicate at payment time: "{S} represents a cost that can be paid with one mana of any type produced by a snow source" (106.11, 107.4h), reduced like generic (118.7g). Persistence is a pool policy: pools empty each step/phase (106.4, turn-based action 703.4q). "Tapped for mana" triggers/replacements key on the production event (106.12a–b).

**Asymmetry.** Exactly one attribute (color/colorlessness) gets first-class type status, cost symbols, and the 118.7 reduction algebra; every other attribute — snow-ness, source identity, spend-predicates, riders — is bolted on as annotations, and only snow ever got a *cost-side* symbol (107.4h). There is no "{legendary-source} mana" symbol, no general "mana produced by an artifact" cost — yet snow proves the cost-side can query source properties, and 106.6 proves the mana-side can carry arbitrary predicates. The asymmetry is pure history: type = what 1993 needed; predicates = what came later and never got promoted.

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: each mana unit is `(type ∈ 6, source_snapshot, spend_predicate, riders[], pool_persistence)` — `engine.md`'s pool gains three fields. Cost symbols compile to *payment predicates over units*: `{R}` = type=red; `{S}` = snow(source_snapshot); a future `{quality}` symbol is the same compile target. The 106.6a replication rule (separate delayed trigger per produced mana) tells you riders must live on the unit, not on the producing effect. Grammar: "Spend this mana only on/to cast ⟨predicate⟩" and "When this mana is spent, …" are attested clause shapes attached to add-instructions — one production each, both predicate-parameterized.

**Danger notes.** Source snapshots vs. LKI: snow-paying checks the *source's* snow-ness at production, and 106.3 routes ability-produced mana's source to the ability's source (113.7) — the snapshot must be taken at add-time or Blood Moon-style type changes corrupt payments. "As though it were any color/type" effects change only how a cost is paid, not the mana (609.4b via `effects.md`) — keep payment-side coercions out of the unit. And the 601.2f cost lock means spend-predicates are evaluated at 601.2g–h payment time, after locking — a unit whose predicate references mutable state ("only if you control a Dragon") needs evaluation-time pinned.

## 14. Entity capability matrix: players and permanents as ad-hoc peers

**Shape.** `capability(entity-class ∈ {player, creature, permanent, card, spell, ability, battle, …}, capability ∈ {be targeted, carry counters, host attachments, take damage, hold guards, be controlled, …})` — the cross-cutting rows-vs-columns view of Families 5–7.

**Instances.** Players already are: damage recipients (120.1), counter carriers (122.1), Aura hosts (702.5d, 303.4), guard carriers for shroud/hexproof/protection (702.18a, 702.11c, 702.16b), targets (115.2's "or a player"), delegation subjects (723), flag carriers (Family 12). Spells/abilities-on-stack are objects (109.1) and can carry counters in practice (Skullbriar, 122.1a's zone-generic wording) and be copy-sources (707.10) and control-subjects (613.1b layer-2 effects are object-generic; 112.2 default).

**Asymmetry.** The matrix is filled opportunistically: players host Auras but not Equipment (301.5); permanents get ward but players don't (702.21a + corpus check: zero player-ward grants); battles can be attacked (310.5) but never attached (310.9); cards (as opposed to permanents) carry only one mid-game attribute (commander, 903.3) and the prepared-adjacent designations; abilities on the stack can be copied (707.10) and countered but carry no flags or counters by any current card. The "any target" shorthand (115.4) is the canonical precedent: the rules invented a *grammar-level name for a row of the matrix* (creature ∪ player ∪ planeswalker ∪ battle) once the row stabilized.

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: define `GameEntity` as the union (players + objects), and make every subsystem from Families 5–7 and 12 take entity arguments rather than per-class methods — the CR's own drafting style already does this ("object or player" appears verbatim in 122.1, 701.3a, 303.4, 616.1). Capability gaps become predicate denials, not missing methods. Grammar: target-class descriptors ("any target", "target player or planeswalker") are closed-class noun phrases over the entity union — `grammar.md` §5 handles them; new matrix cells need vocabulary, not syntax.

**Danger notes.** Players have no characteristics (109.3 lists object characteristics) — capabilities that read characteristics (protection-from-quality checking the *guarded* side, P/T-based requirements) need player-side analogs or explicit denials. Zone-membership is meaningless for players, so capabilities with zone-change policies (counters, 122.2) need a player-side persistence story (player counters persist; the rules never say so explicitly because players never change zones — encode it). Leaving the game is the player analog of zone-change and has bespoke cleanup throughout (725.4, 901.6 succession patterns) — generalized capabilities multiply those succession rules.

## 15. Turn-script editing: insert and delete exist, reorder and reshape don't

**Shape.** `edit_turn_script(op ∈ {insert, delete(replace-with-nothing), reorder, mutate}, granularity ∈ {turn, phase, step, event}, position)`.

**Instances.** Insert: extra turns queued LIFO, APNAP-batched (500.7); extra phases "directly after the specified phase," most-recent-first (500.8); extra steps before/after a specified step (500.9). Delete: skips as replacement effects with counted pending semantics (614.10, 614.10a) and post-skip action scheduling (614.10b). The script itself is fixed: five phases, ordered steps (500.1 via `state.md`).

**Asymmetry.** Insert and delete are mature, parameterized by granularity and position; *reorder* and *mutate* (a combat step in someone else's turn, an upkeep mid-combat, changing whose turn a phase belongs to) have no rules support at all. Precedent: the insert family was itself extended stepwise (turns → phases → steps, 500.7 → 500.8 → 500.9 — three near-identical rules), which is the same one-cell-at-a-time pattern as Family 10, and shows the position/granularity parameters are already real.

**Unified mechanic.** Engine: represent the remaining turn as an explicit script (list of phase/step records with owner annotations), with 500.7–9 as insertions and 614.10 as a counted-deletion replacement watching script-advance events; turn-based actions (703.4 table) key on script-node entry, so script editing automatically reschedules them (614.10a's "anything scheduled for a skipped step … won't happen" falls out). Grammar: "take an extra turn after this one," "additional combat phase," "skip your next untap step" are attested fixed phrases — a position-parameterized production covers all.

**Danger notes.** "Once a step, phase, or turn has started, it can no longer be skipped" (614.10) — deletions apply only to un-entered script nodes; the engine needs a hard cursor boundary. Pending-skip *counting* (614.10a) means deletions are a multiset matched against future occurrences, not script mutations at creation time. Extra-turn LIFO with APNAP insertion (500.7) means the queue is built incrementally during resolution — order of insertion is observable, so the script editor cannot batch.

## Engine modeling directives

Cross-cutting recommendations — generalizations the data model should adopt **now**, with current rules expressed as parameter constraints so that lifting a constraint is data, not surgery:

1. **Make permissions first-class records, not code paths.** Casting/playing legality (Family 1) and timing (Family 3) should be one mechanism: a set of permission entries `(action, object-predicate, zone+owner, window, cost-mode)` evaluated under 601.3's allow-minus-prohibit conjunction. The seven per-type hand rules (301.1–310.1), 117.1a–c, flash (702.8a), and every "cast from" keyword (702.34a, 702.138a, 702.170d, 903.8) are rows in that table.

2. **Separate role from identity in every operation.** Draw must carry `(drawer, library-owner)` even while always equal (121.1 vs. fateseal's 701.29a proves the owner slot is real); attach must carry `(attachment, host, predicate, violation-policy)` (701.3a–b, 704.5m/n/p); triggers must carry a nullable source (725.2/726.2's "no source" inherent triggers). Wherever the CR writes "their/your," store it as a bound parameter.

3. **One event-rewriter with a typed transform algebra.** Implement the 616.1 fixpoint once, with transforms {substitute, null, retarget, scale, shield-decrement} and consumption policies {once-per-event (614.5), N-points (615.7), whole-instance} — prevention (615) and redirection (614.9) are bindings, not subsystems. Keep the 616.1b–d must-choose-first categories as data.

4. **One ProgressTrack type** with edge-triggered (714.2b) and level-sensitive (711.2a) threshold modes, a counters-vs-designation exposure flag (the proliferate boundary, 701.34a vs. 702.87c), and a guarded terminal SBA (714.4/704.5t's pending-trigger clause). Sagas, levelers, Classes, dungeons, the Ring, and speed are six rows.

5. **Counters on any GameEntity, with per-kind zone-function masks and persistence policies.** 122.1's carrier domain is already general; encode 122.1a–b's where-it-functions lists and 122.2's default-cease (with suspend/Skullbriar-style overrides) as fields, and scope counter-verbs (proliferate, bolster) by selector predicates restricted to public zones (the 701.17c line).

6. **One copy primitive** `make_copy(source, dest, lifetime, choice-policy, then)` with 704.5d–e as the default lifetime and 722.3c as the proof that lifetime is overridable. Always copy through copiable values (707.2, post-layer-1 cache per 613.2c).

7. **Route all player choices through a decider-resolution layer** (`decision-kind → actual decider + constraints`), seeded by goad constraints (701.15b–d), protector rebinding (310.8d), retarget grants (707.10c), and whole-player control (723.3/723.5). The 508.1c–d requirement/restriction solver stays generic.

8. **Guards as data** `(interaction, source-predicate, response ∈ {forbid, toll, prevent})` with subsumption (702.11e), compiled to statics (shroud/hexproof/protection) or generated triggers (ward, 702.21a). New ladder rungs (player-ward, attach-tolls) become rows.

9. **Flags via one schema** (carrier-class, multiplicity, gain/loss, copiable?, inherent abilities, payload), seeded from the designations inventory; single-holder steal/succession (725.3–4 pattern) as multiplicity policies. New mechanics' markers become content.

10. **Mana units carry (type, source-snapshot, spend-predicate, riders, persistence)**; cost symbols compile to payment predicates ({S} = 107.4h's source-property check), riders replicate per-unit (106.6a).

11. **The turn is an editable script** with insert (500.7–9) and counted-delete (614.10–614.10a) operations and a hard already-started cursor boundary; turn-based actions hang off script nodes.

12. **Grammar consequence, globally:** almost none of these generalizations needs new syntax. The oracle grammar's existing parameterized productions — keyword + parameter (`grammar.md` §3), `from ⟨zone⟩` complements, entity-union noun phrases (115.4), activation-instruction tails (602.1b) — cover the general forms; the work is in making the *semantic targets* of those productions parameterized records. The one genuinely new production worth adding preemptively is the triggered-ability instruction tail ("Resolve this ability only as a sorcery"), mirroring 602.5d–e, because Family 3 is the seed example most likely to be printed someday — and the delayed-trigger machinery (603.7a) means the engine can implement it the day it appears.
